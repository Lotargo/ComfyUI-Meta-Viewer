# Scenario: landscape_environment

Use for natural landscapes, outdoor environments, establishing panoramic views, and location concepts where terrain, atmosphere, depth layers, weather dynamics, and scale are the main visual subjects.

SECTION 1 — 3-TIER GEOLOCATION & BIOME PRIORS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1.1  REAL-WORLD GEOGRAPHIC ANCHORS
     • Tier 1 (Identifiable Natural Landmark):
       If depicting a famous real-world location, state it with a strong dataset prior
       (e.g., “Yosemite Valley with El Capitan”, “rugged coast of the Amalfi cliffs”).
     • Tier 2 (Regional Biome Prior):
       Anchor generic landscapes to distinct regional ecosystems with coherent flora and geology:
       “misty pine forest and granite cliffs similar to the Pacific Northwest”,
       “dramatic Nordic fjord with sheer rocky precipices and deep reflective glacial waters”,
       “windswept arid canyon reminiscent of the American Southwest”.
     • Tier 3 (Local Terrain Vista):
       Describe specific terrain features without forcing arbitrary geography when a generic scene is desired.

SECTION 2 — ATMOSPHERIC PERSPECTIVE & DEPTH STRUCTURE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

2.1  FOREGROUND-TO-BACKGROUND STRUCTURE
     • Establish a clear 3-tier depth progression:
       - Foreground: crisp textured details, tactile rock surfaces, wild grasses, riverbanks, or scale anchors.
       - Midground: dominant landforms, meandering river courses, ridgelines, forest canopies.
       - Background: distant mountain silhouettes, atmospheric haze, horizon falloff, and dynamic cloud cover.

2.2  ATMOSPHERIC PERSPECTIVE & SCALE
     • Distant elements naturally lose contrast and shift toward cool atmospheric tints.
     • Use natural scale references (lone ancient pine, weathered wooden shelter, winding footpath)
       to communicate majestic scale.

SECTION 3 — WEATHER DYNAMICS & ENVIRONMENTAL LIGHT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

3.1  DYNAMIC WEATHER & MOTION CUES
     • Infuse movement and weather interaction: wind rustling through foliage, ocean spray crashing on sea stacks,
       wisps of morning valley fog, rain showers catching low sunbeams.

3.2  WATER & REFLECTIVE SURFACES
     • Describe water by flow, surface state, clarity, bank contact, and light reflections
       (e.g. “dark glass-like lake surface mirroring illuminated mountain peaks”).

SECTION 4 — CONSTRUCTION RULES & PATTERNS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. Establish the biome and location character, dominant landforms, horizon, and depth hierarchy before small details.
2. Organise the view into readable foreground, middle ground, and background with overlaps, foreground-to-background structure, and scale progression.
3. Keep geography coherent: Water follows terrain, vegetation matches climate, and distant forms obey atmospheric perspective.
4. Translate abstract moods (majestic, serene, desolate, lush) into visible light, weather, palette, and landforms.
5. Name the primary light and weather sources, describing their visible effects across land, sky, and water.
6. Preserve a clear scale system across trees, boulders, rivers, and topography.
7. Keep incidental human structures or figures subordinate unless explicitly requested.

GOOD PATTERN:
`biome and location character + dominant landforms + viewpoint and horizon + foreground-to-background structure + directional river or path + weather and atmospheric perspective + motivated light + scale references + medium and mood`

BAD PATTERN:
`masterpiece landscape, epic mountains, beautiful river, cinematic sky, ultra detailed nature, 8k, best quality`

SELF-CHECK:
- Biome and location character, terrain, season, and time of day are mutually consistent.
- Dominant landforms, horizon, and foreground-to-background structure create readable depth.
- Water courses, terrain, and vegetation follow believable geography.
- Atmospheric perspective and detail density change convincingly with distance.
- Natural lighting and weather create visible effects across sky, terrain, and water.
